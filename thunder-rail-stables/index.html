What happens here

Four ways to be at the barn

Boarding, lessons, training, haul-ins, summer camps, cattle events and a homeschool program — with, in their words, “meticulous and exceptional care with all day turnouts.”

  • Saddleseat, huntseat, western and dressage. All breeds and disciplines are welcome, and the barn takes on problem horses as readily as first-timers.
  • Barrel racing with Jayda. Beginners through advanced, green horses to seasoned. Haul-ins wanted.
  • Three arenas to ride in: a 60 × 120 indoor, a 160 × 200 outdoor, and the 60-foot covered round pen.
  • Call to confirm current lesson times and availability.
  • Three levels of care — full, partial and self — all with all-day turnouts and spacious turnout fields.
  • 12 × 12 matted stalls with individual water systems.
  • Two hot-water wash racks, individual tack lockers and spacious tack rooms.
  • Heated bathroom with washer and dryer, a heated lounge, Wi-Fi, ample parking and a trailer turnaround.
  • 9 am to 12 pm, $75 a day in the 2026 season, for horse-crazy kids ages 5 and up.
  • A day looks like: horseback riding, swimming, roping, obstacles, gaming patterns, animal feeding and care, games, crafts and new friendships.
  • Scholarships. In the barn’s own words: “We believe every child should have the opportunity to experience horse camp.” Thanks to sponsors, limited financial assistance is available — reach out if cost is a concern.
  • The 2026 camp days ran through August 19. Call for next season’s dates.
  • A homeschool club that meets at the barn — riding alongside hands-on time with the animals. It runs as its own club, not a drop-in lesson.
  • Days, ages and this year’s openings change with the season — call to hear what’s running now.

The family behind it

Family owned, operated and lived in

“Nestled in the base of the Cascade foothills in beautiful Enumclaw, Washington, with a stunning view of Mount Rainier.”

Kelly Neu runs Thunder Rail. The care is the part people write about — the barn describes it as meticulous and exceptional, and the reviews that follow tend to agree.

The year runs on its own calendar. Summer camps fill the mornings in July and August, the homeschool club takes over during the school year, cattle events and 4-H clinics book the indoor, and riders haul in from around the plateau just to use the arena for an afternoon.
