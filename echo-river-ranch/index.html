Pick your ride

Half an hour to all day

“We have a huge variety of ride types, from one half hour to all day rides, we’ll even plan an overnight camping trip.” — Echo River Ranch’s own listing

  • The shortest way to find out whether you like being on a horse. Guided the whole way, at a walk, on trails picked for your skill level.

  • Horses are brushed, tacked and ready when you arrive — the ranch charges for time in the saddle, not for the time it takes to get a horse ready.

  • Long enough to get well into the timber and back. Riders call it a relaxing afternoon rather than a lesson — you ride, Debie guides and points things out along the way.

  • Horses can be hauled out to any of the many local trailheads, so a longer ride isn’t limited to what’s within reach of the ranch.

  • In her own words: flat trails through to high back country in the mountains with amazing scenic views. The route gets matched to the riders, not the other way round.

  • The ranch will plan an overnight camping trip on horseback. It’s built around your group rather than run to a schedule, so start with a text about the dates you have in mind.

  • The ranch sponsors egg hunt, trick-n-treat, candy cane, patriotic and sweetheart rides. In fall Debie runs rides that take in wild mushroom and berry foraging — she can name the native plants and edible berries as you pass them. Ask which one is coming up next.

  • Family and small-group rides for birthdays, campfires, lunches and dinners, picnics, scavenger hunts and wine tasting where age-appropriate. There are also casual bonfire meet-and-greets and basic horse introduction sessions for anyone who wants to be around horses before they get on one.

For rides without a listed price, ask Debie for a quote before booking.

The guide

Meet your trail guide

Debie grew up in Washington and has been riding here for over forty years — she has helped clear and maintain a good many of the trails you’ll be on.

That’s the difference between a trail ride and a trail ride with her. She knows the terrain, and along the way she can point out the native plants and the berries worth eating. In fall she runs rides built around wild mushroom and berry foraging.

The ranch is family-owned, five acres about five miles northeast of Enumclaw. There are only a few horses, so groups stay small — one to four riders, walk only, no loping and no running. She specialises in confidence building, which is why so many of the reviews are from people who had never sat on a horse before. Missy, the ranch’s riding dog, turns up in a lot of riders’ photos.

Reserve a ride

Your ride starts with a text

  1. Text Debie

    Send the day you have in mind and how many riders. Texting is the way she takes reservations.
