Pick your ride

Half an hour to all day

“We have a huge variety of ride types, from one half hour to all day rides, we’ll even plan an overnight camping trip.” — Echo River Ranch’s own listing

  • The shortest way to find out whether you like being on a horse. Guided the whole way, at a walk, on trails picked for your skill level.

  • An hour in the saddle, walk only — no loping, no running. Riders who had never sat on a horse before start here.

  • Long enough to get well into the timber and back. One rider put it this way: “Ever have that great friend that says come on over and you just have a relaxing time.”

  • Horses get hauled out to any of the many local trailheads, so a longer ride isn’t limited to what’s within reach of the ranch.

  • In her own words: flat trails through to high back country in the mountains with amazing scenic views. The route gets matched to the riders, not the other way round.

  • The ranch will plan an overnight camping trip on horseback. It’s built around your group rather than run to a schedule, so start with a text about the dates you have in mind.

  • The ranch sponsors egg hunt, trick-n-treat, candy cane, patriotic and sweetheart rides. In fall she runs rides built around wild mushroom and berry foraging. Ask which one is coming up next.

  • Family and small-group rides for birthdays, campfires, lunches and dinners, picnics and scavenger hunts. There are also bonfire meet-and-greets and basic horse introduction sessions for anyone who wants to be around horses before they get on one.

Groups are one to four riders, walk only. For a ride without a listed price, ask Debie for a quote before you book.

The guide

Meet your trail guide

Debie grew up in Washington and has been riding here for over forty years. She has helped clear and maintain a good many of the trails you’ll be on.

That’s the difference between a trail ride and a trail ride with her. She knows the terrain, and along the way she can name the native plants and the berries worth eating. In fall she runs rides built around wild mushroom and berry foraging.

The ranch is family-owned. There are only a few horses, so groups stay small and every ride is walk only — she specialises in confidence building, which is why so many of the reviews come from people who had never sat on a horse before. Missy, the ranch’s riding dog, turns up in a lot of riders’ photos.
