The story

One pair of hands, one growing food forest

Johann's Garden is a one-person operation "borne out of passion for gardening and food" — a nursery whose plants are raised by the same hands, in the same dirt, as the forest around them.

The display tables aren't in a parking lot — they stand in the garden itself, on land the owner names plainly: this nursery grows "on Lushootseed Salish land," and its catalog carries those older plant names beside the Latin. Every description is written from firsthand, localized experience — what this exact plant did in this exact county.

"As a small nursery, I could go one of two major routes… focus on growing a huge diversity of plants, but only produce each in limited quantities to maximize the selection available. I chose option #2." — the grower, on how this nursery works
  • Pesticides avoided by growing locally-adapted plants in the first place
  • Gravity-fed irrigation to keep the energy bill near zero
  • Durable grow trays and nursery pots reused season after season
  • Biodegradable pots where pots can't be reused
