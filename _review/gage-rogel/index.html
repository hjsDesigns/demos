His story

The skinny kid. The lifter who bulked too far. The guy who looked fit but didn’t feel well.

Gage has been all three. WSU Kinesiology. He coaches so his clients can skip the years of trial and error it took him to learn it.

His mother studied natural medicine and raised the family to be deliberate about food and lifestyle. Since then he has applied that lesson to himself: testing training methods, nutrition strategies, routines and supplements, tracking the results, keeping what worked and discarding what didn’t.

Read the whole story

01 · High schoolThe bulk

A small, skinny kid who was unhappy with the mirror. Between his freshman and junior years he grew from 5′1″ and 95 pounds to 5′10″ and 215 pounds: nine inches and 120 pounds in roughly two years. The work was consistent and the progress undeniable, but chasing a number on the scale meant a lot of body fat came with it. He had learned how to train hard and eat to grow. He had not yet learned how to do it well.

02 · Freshman year of collegeThe cut

This time the goal was a look and a feeling instead of a number. Over the first five months of freshman year he brought his bodyweight from 215 down to 185: 30 pounds at roughly a pound and a half a week. He followed the fundamentals closely: a consistent protein target, tracked calories and macros, and regular cardio. He kept the muscle and was lean for the first time in years. On paper, everything right.

03 · CollegeThe wake-up call

The mirror and how he felt told two different stories. Ongoing digestive problems, persistent brain fog, one cold or flu after another in dorm living. His physique had never looked better; his health was clearly not where it needed to be. That period changed how he thinks about fitness. Like many people in the fitness world, he had relied on supplements, adding another whenever something felt off. They were covering gaps the diet should have filled. Tracking had made him lean. It had not made him healthy.

04 · Years of researchThe blueprint

So he went back to where the interest started: the quality of the food, and how different foods affected his digestion, energy and clarity. Years of trial and error, including plenty of popular diets that didn’t hold up. The same discipline went into training: writing, testing and refining his own programs, tracking every working set, paying attention to what produced results and what only produced fatigue. Everything he learned came together into one complete system. It is the blueprint he uses for his own health and physique, and the foundation of every program he builds. What’s inside it is reserved for the people he coaches.

Questions

What people ask Gage before they apply.

How long does a coaching phase run?

A phase usually runs about 12 weeks. At the end of a phase we go over your trackers together and build the next one from there.

Do you write generic meal plans?

No. Your meals come from your MyFitnessPal, the foods you already like, and anything you don’t eat or can’t have. If you’re not a breakfast person, your plan won’t make you eat breakfast.
