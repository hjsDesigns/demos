What’s here

Everything on the farm, one tap at a time

Explore attractions from the farm’s recent seasons. Confirm this year’s lineup and admission before visiting.

  • Twenty-plus varieties out in the field — orange, white, warty, tiny. Walk the rows, pick the one you want, and pay by its size on the way out.

  • Four acres cut fresh into the standing corn every season. The aerial below shows its 2024 design.

  • A towering stack of bales to climb, with the tube slide running down the side of it.

  • A pumpkin the size of a shed, and it bounces. Shoes off at the edge.

  • Football, baseball and basketball inside the barn. Corn hole and giant Jenga out on the grass.

  • Bubbles the size of a person, pulled up off the truck bed when the light goes gold.

  • Kettle corn popped on site and honey from local hives, both at the stand. Fall decor is out for sale beside them.

  • The farm sets up photo spots through the season — pumpkins, corn stalks and the mountain doing most of the work.

The farm

Run by the Williams family

A family farm on SE 464th that opens its fields to the rest of Enumclaw every fall.

The pumpkins are grown here, the maze is cut into their own corn, and the launcher, the hay pyramid and the bounce house go up for the season and come down again when the last pumpkin leaves.

Visitors have shared stories of family outings and birthday parties here, with the fields and Mount Rainier behind them.

How to find the farm

  1. Confirm your day

    Call for this year’s opening dates, gate hours and arrival details before driving out.

  2. Ask about admission

    Check current prices, which attractions are included and how pumpkins are priced.

  3. Bring the wagon

    Wear boots you don’t mind. It is a working field, and it is usually wet by mid-October.
