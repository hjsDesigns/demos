What’s here

A working farm, open for the fall

Everything below is theirs, photographed on the place. Call to confirm which of it is running this year before you drive out.

  • Pick your own

    Rows of them still on the vine — orange, white, warty, tiny. Walk out, pick the one you want, pay for it on the way back.

  • The four-acre corn maze

    Cut fresh into their own corn every season. Rules and a riddle board hang at the gate before you go in.

  • Pyramid slides

    Straw bales stacked into steps, a tower at the top and two tube slides down the front. The sign over it is hand-painted like everything else.

  • The Red Barn

    A jack-o’-lantern the size of a shed, parked in the barn out of the rain. Yard games and the sports barn are in there too.

  • Pumpkin Snatchers

    Three holes in a board, their name across the front. That’s the picture everybody takes home.

  • A fire by the corn

    A stone ring, a farm bench with their name on it, and a wagon parked beside it, right up against the corn.

  • Gourds and fall decor

    Bins of them by the crate — striped, warty, knuckled. Mums and corn stalks sit out front beside the market.

  • Take a wagon

    They’re parked by the gate and out at the fire ring. The big ones are not carrying themselves back.

  • Finding your way

    Every arrow on the barn corner is hand-lettered: pumpkin shop, restrooms, corn maze, pyramid slides, market, red barn fun, pick-your-own patch.

The farm

Run by the Williams family

A working farm on SE 464th that opens its fields to the rest of Enumclaw every fall.

The pumpkins are grown here. The maze is cut into their own corn. The slides, the fire ring and the big inflatable pumpkin go up for the season and come down again when the last pumpkin leaves.

Their name is on the sign at the gate, on the bench by the fire and on the photo board in the grass — and every one of those signs was painted by hand.

How to find the farm

  1. Confirm the day

    Call for this year’s opening dates and gate hours before you drive out — they run weekends only, and the season is short.

  2. Ask what it costs

    Admission and pumpkin pricing are set at the gate each year. Ask what’s included while you have them on the phone.

  3. Wear boots

    It’s a working field, and it is usually wet by mid-October. Wagons are there for the heavy ones.
