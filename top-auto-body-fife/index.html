Who's behind the counter

Under new ownership

The reviews written since the shop changed hands name the new owner as Cesar.

That matters more here than it would somewhere else. A body shop is a business you use once, in a bad week, on somebody else's schedule — and the only thing you have to go on is whether the last hundred people came out of it happy.

The building says something too. Charcoal siding, one blue stripe carried the whole way round it, a bay door painted to match the sign, and a neon OPEN in the window. Inside there's a front office rather than a parts window: polished concrete, a long counter built out of weathered timber, a couch, and the job tickets racked on the wall behind the desk where you can see them.

Getting it looked at

Pull in off the highway

  1. Ring first

    Call the shop before you drive over, so somebody's at the counter when you pull in rather than out in the bay.

  2. Point at the damage

    Park on the gravel and walk round the car with them. If it isn't driveable, photos on your phone are enough to start with.

  3. Leave it with them

    It goes back out on the same gravel with the panel straight — that's the pair of photographs further up this page.
