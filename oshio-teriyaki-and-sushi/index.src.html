Two things worth knowing

Where this plate came from.

照り焼きTeri · yaki

The name is the recipe.

Teri means the shine — the gloss a sweet soy glaze leaves behind. Yaki means grilled. Put together, the word describes exactly what's happening on the flat top: meat over fire, sauce brushed on until it gleams.

Japanese, 照り焼き

1976Lower Queen Anne

Seattle invented this plate.

Grilled meat, a scoop of rice, a green salad on the side — that combination isn't from Japan, it's from up the road. Toshi Kasahara opened Toshi's Teriyaki on Roy Street in March 1976: five items on the menu, chicken plate $1.85. The style spread across the Puget Sound and took the region's name. Oshio serves that plate.

The house sauce

Made here.
Not poured from a jug.

Both sauces are ours — the teriyaki glaze that goes on the grill, and the katsu that comes alongside anything breaded. It's the thing people write about years after the meal, and the reason a plate here doesn't taste like a plate anywhere else in town.
