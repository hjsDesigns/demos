The stand on Canyon Road

A real cabin, not a kiosk

Cedar shingles, a green metal roof, a river-rock base, black lantern sconces and a cut-out sign with an elk standing in the coffee mug.

Most drive-thrus are a box in a parking lot. This one was built to look like something you'd find at the end of a logging road — timber trusses in the gable, string lights along the eaves, evergreens crowded up behind it. The reader board out by the road gets its letters changed by hand.

It's a two-window stand run by a small crew, and the regulars are the whole business. People who've been pulling in for years, firefighters on shift, the same order most mornings. Ask for a punch card on your first stop.

Stopping by

Three turns and you're there

  1. Pull in off Canyon Road

    The cabin sits back behind the reader board, with the drive lane wrapping around it.

  2. Order at the window

    Hot, iced, blended, energy — it's all on the boards bolted to the side of the building.

  3. Tap, swipe or hand over cash

    Cards, debit and tap-to-pay all work at the window.
